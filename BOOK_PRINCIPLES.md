
# Book Principles

These principles govern the book's content, reasoning, examples, and writing.

## 1. First principles before tools

Start with the problem.

Explain why a capability is needed before introducing the technology that provides it.

## 2. Concepts before terminology

Introduce jargon only when it adds precision.

Never assume that naming a concept means explaining it.

## 3. Mechanism before assertion

Explain how something works and why it works.

Avoid statements that ask the reader to accept a conclusion without understanding its mechanism.

## 4. Examples are part of the explanation

Every chapter must have a meaningful example that exposes its central mechanism.

Where useful, add a failure case and a transfer example.

## 5. Explain limitations honestly

Every important concept has boundaries.

Explain:
- Assumptions.
- Dependencies.
- Trade-offs.
- Failure modes.
- Situations where an alternative works better.

## 6. Separate facts from interpretation

Distinguish:
- Established facts.
- Reasonable interpretations.
- Assumptions.
- Contested claims.
- Speculation.

Verify important factual and historical claims.

## 7. Explain evolution without implying inevitability

Technologies emerge in response to problems and constraints.

New approaches do not automatically make older approaches obsolete.

Explain what changed, why it changed, and what remains useful.

## 8. Avoid false equivalences

Keep distinctions clear:

- Information is not automatically knowledge.
- Data is not automatically evidence.
- Correlation is not causation.
- Prediction is not explanation.
- Intelligence is not judgement.
- A recommendation is not a decision.
- A generated answer is not necessarily true.
- Feedback is not automatically learning.
- Automation is not automatically autonomy.

## 9. Think in connected systems

Explain relationships between concepts.

A chapter should connect backwards to prerequisites and forwards to the next question.

## 10. Use technical depth selectively

Use mathematics, code, architecture, and technical detail when they improve understanding.

Do not introduce complexity merely to demonstrate expertise.

## 11. Preserve intellectual honesty

Do not force every technology into the central framework.

If a concept does not fit neatly, explain why.

Challenge the framework when evidence demands it.

## 12. Avoid filler

Every section must do at least one of the following:

- Answer an important question.
- Explain a mechanism.
- Clarify a distinction.
- Reveal a limitation.
- Connect concepts.
- Prepare the reader for a subsequent idea.

## 13. Chapter continuity contract

Every chapter must answer:

1. What question are we resolving?
2. What should the reader understand now?
3. Why does that understanding matter?
4. What remains unresolved?
5. Why does the next question follow?

## 14. The reader test

After reading a chapter, the reader should be able to:

- Explain the central concept in plain language.
- Describe its mechanism.
- Give an example.
- Identify a limitation.
- Connect it to another concept.
- Apply the idea to an unfamiliar situation.

## 15. AI-assisted writing standard

AI can help research, challenge, structure, review, and improve explanations.

It must not replace the author's responsibility for:

- Accuracy.
- Reasoning.
- Original synthesis.
- Intellectual honesty.
- Final editorial judgement.

The result must sound like a thoughtful human explaining something they genuinely understand.
