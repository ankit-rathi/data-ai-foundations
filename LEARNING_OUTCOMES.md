# Learning Outcomes

These outcomes describe capabilities, not facts to memorise.

Readers should progressively move from understanding individual concepts to reasoning about connected systems and unfamiliar technologies.

## Part I — Understanding Reality

Readers should be able to:

- Distinguish observation, measurement, representation and interpretation.
- Explain why measurements and models are partial and uncertain.
- Recognise misleading patterns, bias and unsupported conclusions.
- Explain why knowledge needs structure and context.

## Part II — Representing and Processing Information

Readers should be able to:

- Explain how information is encoded and processed by machines.
- Describe algorithms, computation, abstraction and basic complexity.
- Understand conceptually how software, operating systems and networks support computation.
- Recognise interfaces, protocols and basic security boundaries.

## Part III — Building Systems Around Data

Readers should be able to:

- Explain how data is modelled, stored, changed and queried.
- Distinguish operational and analytical workloads.
- Reason about transactions, concurrency, distribution and failure.
- Describe the data lifecycle through ingestion, transformation, storage and use.
- Recognise the roles of quality, metadata, lineage, ownership, governance and security.

## Part IV — Turning Data into Intelligence

Readers should be able to:

- Interpret statistical evidence and uncertainty at a foundational level.
- Distinguish association, prediction and causation.
- Explain the basic learning process behind machine learning and modern AI.
- Understand representations, neural networks, language models and retrieval conceptually.
- Evaluate model and AI outputs using suitable evidence and limitations.

## Part V — From Intelligence to Decisions and Action

Readers should be able to:

- Distinguish information quality, prediction quality and decision quality.
- Reason about objectives, uncertainty, risk and trade-offs.
- Explain how decision-support systems influence human behaviour.
- Distinguish automation from autonomy and identify agentic-system risks.
- Assess trust, oversight, accountability and feedback.

## Part VI — Navigating an Uncertain Future

Readers should be able to:

- Separate enduring principles from changing implementations.
- Evaluate technology claims against problems, evidence, costs and risks.
- Recognise system-level effects, dependencies and unintended consequences.
- Use foundational concepts to investigate unfamiliar technologies independently.

## Overall capability

A reader completing this journey should be able to encounter an unfamiliar data or AI development, identify the problem it addresses, investigate its mechanism, question its assumptions, evaluate evidence and trade-offs, and reason about its consequences without relying solely on vendor explanations or current hype.
