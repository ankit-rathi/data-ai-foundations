# Content Principles

## 1. Purpose

Build durable understanding of ideas connecting data, computation, software, statistics, AI and decisions.

The goal is transferable understanding, not exhaustive specialist training.

## 2. Depth philosophy

Assign each concept an intentional depth.

### Level 1 — Awareness
Understand:
- What it is.
- Why it exists.
- Where it is used.
- Which related concepts it supports.

### Level 2 — Working Understanding
Understand:
- How it works.
- Its underlying mechanism.
- Important assumptions.
- Key limitations.
- Relationships with other concepts.

### Level 3 — Critical Understanding
Understand:
- Design choices.
- Trade-offs.
- Failure modes.
- Evaluation approaches.
- Practical and organisational consequences.

Depth should be proportional to the importance of the concept.

Supporting subjects should receive only the depth needed to make dependent concepts understandable.

## 3. Editorial principles

1. First principles before tools: explain the problem before the implementation.
2. Mechanism over terminology: a definition is not an explanation.
3. Connection over memorisation: show meaningful relationships.
4. Capabilities and limitations together.
5. Evidence over hype: distinguish established principles, current practice and speculation.
6. Examples across domains.
7. Separate enduring concepts from current products and frameworks.
8. No artificial completeness: add content to close genuine learning gaps.
9. No filler: every paragraph, diagram and example should improve understanding.
10. Plain language without technical distortion.

## 4. Eight reasoning lenses

Consider these questions when developing every important capsule.

### Reality
What real-world phenomenon or problem are we addressing?

### Representation
What have we represented, and what have we left out?

### Mechanism
How does it actually work?

### Evidence
What supports the claims, and how reliable is it?

### Assumptions
What must be true for it to work?

### Trade-offs
What do we gain, sacrifice or make harder?

### Failure
How can it fail, and how would we know?

### Consequences
Who or what is affected?

These are quality lenses, not mandatory headings in every capsule.

## 5. Capsule types

### Foundation
Introduces one essential concept.

### Connecting
Explains relationships between concepts.

### Synthesis
Combines multiple concepts through a system, case or problem.

## 6. Chapter continuity contract

A chapter should enable readers to:

1. Explain its fundamental idea in their own words.
2. Explain why the concept exists.
3. Describe its mechanism at the intended depth.
4. Identify an assumption or limitation.
5. Connect it to earlier and later ideas.
6. Apply it to an unfamiliar example.
7. Understand why the next chapter follows.

## 7. Technical accuracy

- Define terms when first needed.
- Distinguish facts, interpretations, examples and opinions.
- Explain where an analogy stops being accurate.
- Do not present tool-specific behaviour as a universal principle.
- Date or qualify claims that may change.
- Prefer primary or authoritative sources for technical claims.
- Ensure diagrams are consistent with explanations.
- Disclose meaningful simplifications.

## 8. Learning principles

Use:
- Relatable examples.
- Comparisons.
- Thought experiments.
- Practical scenarios.
- Questions that test understanding.
- Unfamiliar-technology challenges.

Test whether readers can reason with a concept, not merely recall a phrase.

## 9. Visual principles

Use visuals to clarify:
- Mechanisms.
- Relationships.
- Comparisons.
- System boundaries.
- Feedback loops.
- Failure modes.

Avoid decorative diagrams that add no explanatory value.

## 10. What this project is not

- A catalogue of tools or vendor tutorials.
- An attempt to make every reader a specialist in every discipline.
- A collection of disconnected social media posts.
- A promise that fundamentals remove the need for continuous learning.
